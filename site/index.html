Round 1 — Ogrua, member state

"Ogrua notes the seriousness of the reports emerging from Sastos and would caution against language that ventures into the territory of punitive economic measures at this early and still-unclear stage."

Actual red line (never shown to the drafter)
No sanctions language of any kind. sanctions = 0. Non-negotiable.

This is the entire test. A model that reads "caution" as a soft, negotiable preference will draft past the actual constraint — and only discover the mistake when the state holds firm at the final round.

What this tests

Design principle

Hidden positions, diplomatic surface

Every red line is voiced in register, not stated as a condition. The gap between the two is what's being measured.

Design principle

Silence isn't acceptance

A member that says nothing hasn't necessarily agreed. Reading silence as consent, then getting contradicted later, is a scoreable failure.

Design principle

Red lines never cancel out

One violated red line fails the text — regardless of how well six other members' positions were satisfied.

Design principle

Necessary vs. gratuitous dilution

A concession made under real pressure is different from one nobody asked for. Most scoring approaches can't tell them apart. This one does.

Protocol

The scenario, in full

The organisation. The Regional Cooperation Union (RCU) — seven invented member states, with full consensus required for any joint statement drafted and issued by the Secretary-General on all members' behalf.

The trigger. A disputed election in the Republic of Sastos, a non-member state, followed by a lethal crackdown on protesters in a region that holds most of Sastos's rare-earth reserves. Casualty figures are contested and rising. Partway through the negotiation, a second, larger incident occurs — testing whether the model revises a fact it already drafted around, or leaves it stale to avoid reopening a settled paragraph.

Round 1 — Pepryae, member state

"Pepryae would ask that any reference to an ongoing or standing mechanism of oversight directed at Sastos be considered carefully rather than included as a matter of course."

Actual red line
No standing monitoring mechanism targeting Sastos. A time-bound or ad hoc review is acceptable; an open-ended standing mechanism is non-negotiable.

The drafter's own interest

The Secretary-General was elected on human dignity and the rule of law as founding values — and is separately responsible for keeping the Union itself intact. His red line isn't a position on any one dimension: a statement silent across every substantive dimension fails his own mandate, even if all seven members would sign it. Consensus on a null statement is not, for him, a success condition. Most consensus-drafting evals treat the drafter as a neutral aggregator. This one doesn't.

Scorer validation

Does the scoring logic actually work?

Before this protocol is used to compare any models, its scoring layers were checked against six deliberately constructed and hand-scored test cases with expected outcomes — a strong near-consensus compromise, a hollow one, one that's substantive but fails, a buried red-line violation, and two process failures. No model has been run through this scenario yet. This tests whether the rubric discriminates between important outcome shapes; it is not a leaderboard.

Example A 6/7 accept Osnesal — red line
"...calls for a transparent domestic investigation, with international observation, and for the release of any individuals detained without due process..."

The strongest realistic outcome in the set — accepted by six of seven states and clears the Secretary-General's floor — and it still isn't consensus. It falls just short of Osnesal's attribution red line. A scorer that called this "excellent, consensus reached" would be wrong. That distinction is the point.

Example C Highest raw scores 4/7 accept
"...strongly condemns... calls for an independent international investigation... will consider further measures, including sanctions..."

The highest policy-dimension scores of any example in the set — and the worst acceptance outcome. It crosses Ogrua's sanctions red line outright and trips Ogrea's wariness of generalised language. Ranking by substance alone would rate this above Example A. It shouldn't be.

Is consensus possible?

Feasibility control 7/7 possible 0 red-line violations

Yes — in the main scenario. The seven states' acceptance ranges are designed to contain a narrow area of overlap. A successful draft must land inside that overlap while retaining enough substance to clear the Secretary-General's own floor. The impossible variant deliberately removes that overlap; there, the correct result is to identify the unresolved conflict rather than claim consensus.

Why this matters

Adoption is running ahead of evaluation.

A July 2026 Belfer Center review screened close to 80,000 papers on AI capabilities and found twelve evaluating AI performance in real-world statecraft contexts. Meanwhile, consensus-drafting on behalf of multilateral institutions is exactly the kind of task frontier models are already well-suited to being pointed at — precisely because it looks, on the surface, like a writing task. It isn't. It's a task defined by what everyone will actually accept, voiced in language built to obscure that.

What this proves is buildable

One fully worked scenario: eight scored policy dimensions, seven member profiles with genuinely different reasons to hold their positions, a drafter with an institutional stake distinct from any member's, a silence rule, a fact-currency test, and a validated scoring rubric that correctly separates acceptance from substance, and necessary concessions from gratuitous ones — checked against six hand-scored cases before any model touches it.

What it doesn't claim

CONSENSUS is a protocol and one fully worked scenario, not a comparative model study. It does not claim that one synthetic negotiation captures diplomacy as a whole, that its current hand-scored set constitutes independent validation, or that a single run establishes model reliability. The skeletal Inspect harness shows the intended experimental shape; it is not presented as a production benchmark.

A credible next phase would add several scenario families, an independently validated LLM judge from a different model family than any system under test, a small practitioner baseline, repeated runs, and robustness checks against superficial changes such as state ordering, comment tone, and comment length. Those checks are necessary to distinguish sensitivity to negotiating substance from sensitivity to surface pattern.
